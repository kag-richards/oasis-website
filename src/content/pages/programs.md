Self sufficiency is not a single problem with a single fix. A woman can have a bed and still lose her job because she has no way to get there. She can have a car and still relapse because nothing addressed the trauma underneath. Our program is built to close all four gaps at once.

## 1. Transitional Housing

Safe, sober housing for women who have completed a drug or alcohol recovery program.

Residents stay 12 to 24 months while they work toward stability and independence. During that time they receive case management, life skills coaching, support groups, and direct assistance with employment and permanent housing placement.

The home will accommodate up to 8 residents, with 4 bedrooms, shared living areas, a kitchen, and dedicated space for case management. Residents pay a nominal program fee of $400 per month, far below market rent, charged only to help offset operating costs. No profit is taken from these fees, and fees are reduced or waived when a resident cannot afford them.

## 2. Transportation Assistance

The barrier that quietly undoes everything else.

We provide bus passes, ride share vouchers, gas cards, and, where possible, staff and volunteer drivers. That covers travel to work, medical and mental health appointments, court and probation meetings, childcare, education and training programs, and recovery support meetings.

No repayment is required.

## 3. Educational Support

Financial literacy is where we start, because managing money is the skill that determines whether independence lasts.

Beyond that, we provide job readiness training including resume writing, interview skills, and workplace expectations, plus tutoring, computer skills training, and referrals to local colleges and workforce programs. Core educational services are free to participants.

## 4. Mental Health Resources

Addressing what sits underneath.

Services include peer support groups, mental health screenings, crisis intervention, referrals to licensed outside providers, and psychoeducational workshops on coping skills, trauma, stress management, and relapse prevention. Services are delivered by licensed professionals and trained peer support specialists.

Services are offered at no cost or on a sliding scale. No one is turned away for inability to pay.

## How a woman enters the program

<div class="quiet-note">
We are currently pre-house. The intake process below describes how the program operates once our first home opens.
</div>

**Referral or application.** Women come to us directly, or through shelters, social service agencies, hospitals, faith communities, and other community partners. Each applicant completes an intake form covering housing status, income, safety concerns, and support needs.

**Intake assessment.** Staff conduct an interview, in person or by phone, to verify housing instability, review financial hardship, assess safety concerns, and determine whether our program is the right fit for her needs.

**Prioritization.** When there are more eligible applicants than beds, priority goes to women in immediate danger or fleeing violence, women who are literally homeless, and women with the fewest alternative options.

**Admission and ongoing support.** Accepted residents receive an orientation to house rules, program expectations, and available services, followed by regular case management meetings and an individualized service plan.

If we cannot accept an eligible applicant because of space or fit, we provide referrals to other appropriate community resources.
